The gap

Today's tools miss the most dangerous trackers

Phones

Phones warn about Bluetooth tags like AirTag, but cannot see SIM-card GPS trackers.

Cheap bug detectors

How it works

Five steps from signal to source

  1. Step 1

    Listens to uplink bands only

    Listens only to uplink bands (880–915, 832–862, 1710–1785 MHz), so a strong signal means a transmitter is close.

  2. Step 2

    Recognises GSM

    Recognises GSM by its 217 Hz burst pattern.

  3. Step 3

    Tells a tracker from a phone

    Tells a tracker from a phone by its even reporting rhythm.

  4. Step 4

    Guides to the source, quietly

    Guides to the source with a “hot-cold” vibration, quietly.

  5. Step 5

    Also finds Bluetooth tags

    Also finds AirTag, Tile and SmartTag.

Evidence

Every finding becomes evidence

Every finding goes into a hash-chained log: each entry holds the SHA-256 hash of the previous one. The log is signed by an ATECC608 secure chip.

One button exports a signed PDF report for the police.
